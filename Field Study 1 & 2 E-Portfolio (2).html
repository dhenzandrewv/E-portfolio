Introduction

Choosing to become a teacher means preparing myself not only to teach lessons but also to understand, guide, and inspire students. As a Bachelor of Secondary Education major in Science, I know that becoming a good teacher takes more than knowing the subject. It requires patience, confidence, creativity, and the willingness to keep learning. I still have many things to improve, but I believe that every experience I gain brings me one step closer to becoming the teacher I want to be.

Through Field Study 1 and Field Study 2, I hope to learn more about what really happens inside a classroom. I want to understand how students learn, how teacher/s handle different situations, and how they make lessons meaningful and engaging. I also want to observe how my cooperating teacher prepares lessons, manages the class, and responds to the different needs of learners. These experiences will help me connect what I have learned in college with the actual responsibilities of being a teacher.

For me, Field Study is more than a requirement to complete my degree. It’s an opportunity to discover my strengths, recognize the areas where I need to improve, and prepare myself for the challenges of the teaching profession. I know that I may still face difficulties along the way, but I’m willing to learn from them and become better through experience and guidance.

Someday, I hope to become a Science teacher who doesn’t simply explain scientific concepts but also encourages students to ask questions, think critically, and develop a genuine interest in learning. More than anything, I want to become the kind of teacher who makes a positive difference in the lives of my students.

Brief Biography

My name is Dhenz Andrew P. Valenzuela, and I’m a fourth-year Bachelor of Secondary Education major in Science at Taguig City University. I consider myself a creative person who enjoys making original instructional materials and finding new ways to make learning more engaging. For me, teaching shouldn’t be limited to explaining lessons and asking students to memorize information. I want them to enjoy what they’re learning and have experiences they’ll still remember even when they grow older. I also believe that students learn in different ways, so I want to explore various teaching strategies that suit their needs, interests, and learning styles. Beyond Science, I’m interested in learning about other fields, too, because I want to share knowledge that can help my students better understand the world around them.

I chose teaching because I believe teachers play an important role in shaping future professionals and helping build a better society. I chose Science as my major because I know that many students find it difficult or see it as a subject that’s hard to enjoy. I want to change that by making Science more relatable, interesting, and easier to appreciate through creative instructional materials and teaching strategies. I want my students to do more than memorize facts for exams. I want them to ask questions, think critically, analyze information, and use what they’ve learned in real-life situations. My goal is to become a teacher who makes learning meaningful, encourages students to discover what they’re capable of, and inspires them to keep learning even beyond the classroom.

Personal Educational Philosophy

I believe teaching is more than standing in front of a class and explaining lessons. It’s about helping students learn in ways that make sense to them and giving them experiences they can carry with them even after they’ve left the classroom. Every student learns differently, so I believe teachers shouldn’t rely on just one teaching strategy. They need to understand their students and find ways to make learning more engaging and enjoyable. As a future Science teacher, I love creating my own instructional materials and finding creative ways to bring lessons to life. I don’t want my students to simply memorize facts for an exam and forget them afterward. I want them to enjoy the process of learning, stay curious, and remember the things they’ve experienced in my class, even when they’re older.

I also believe that learning shouldn’t stop at understanding concepts. Students need to know how to question what they learn, analyze information, solve problems, and apply their knowledge to real-life situations. For me, being a good teacher doesn’t mean knowing everything. It means being willing to learn, improve, and adjust to what students need. I chose this profession because I believe teachers help shape the people who will eventually contribute to society in different ways. I know teaching can be exhausting, and there will be days when things don’t go as planned. Still, I want to remind myself why I chose this path in the first place. My goal is to make Science less intimidating and more enjoyable for my students, help them see what they’re capable of, and become the kind of teacher whose lessons and encouragement they’ll still remember long after they’ve left my classroom.

Career Objectives

Teaching is more than sharing knowledge. It’s about guiding students, helping them think critically, and building their confidence to face challenges. As a Future Licensed Professional Teacher major in Science, I aim to secure a teaching position where I can use my creative skills, leadership experience, and learner-centered approach to make learning meaningful and engaging. In the short term, I hope to create a supportive classroom where students feel encouraged to learn and grow. In the long term, I want to continue developing my teaching skills and become an educator who makes a positive and lasting difference in students’ lives.

Learning Goals

My personal learning goals and expectations for Field Study 1 and Field Study 2 are focused on understanding the realities of teaching and preparing myself for my future role as an educator.

Field Study 1

My goal is to understand how students learn and how the classroom environment affects their behavior and participation in TNHS. I want to observe how the teacher/s manage their classes, handle different learning needs, and create a comfortable space where students feel encouraged to learn. As a future Science teacher, I also want to learn how to keep students engaged and make lessons easier to understand and apply in real-life situations.

Field Study 2

My goal is to learn how my cooperating teacher prepares and delivers lessons, uses instructional materials, and assesses students’ learning. I want to observe different teaching strategies and understand how my cooperating teacher explains lessons, manages classroom activities, and checks whether students understand the topics. I also hope to apply what I learned in Field Study 1 and connect the theories I studied in college with actual classroom experiences.

Expectation

By the end of both field studies, I hope to have a deeper understanding of the teaching profession and the responsibilities that come with it. I expect to learn from actual classroom experiences, recognize areas where I need to improve, and develop the patience, confidence, and skills I’ll need to become an effective and responsible science teacher.

Cooperating School Profile

School Profile

Taguig National High School (TNHS) · School ID 305460. A public secondary school along Imelda Romualdez Marcos Road, corner A. Reyes Street, Barangay New Lower Bicutan, Taguig City. Established on January 8, 1980, it was formerly known as Fort Bonifacio College Annex before becoming Taguig Municipal High School. In 1994, it adopted its present name, Taguig National High School. The school provides secondary education and aims to support students’ academic development and personal growth.

Vision

“We dream of Filipinos who passionately love their country and whose values and competencies enable them to realize their full potential and contribute meaningfully to building the nation. As a learner-centered public institution, the Department of Education continuously improves itself to better serve its stakeholders.”

Mission

“To protect and promote the right of every Filipino to quality, equitable, culture-based, and complete basic education where:

  • Students learn in a child-friendly, gender-sensitive, safe, and motivating environment. Teachers facilitate learning and constantly nurture every learner.
  • Administrators and staff, as stewards of the institution, ensure an enabling and supportive environment for effective learning to happen.
  • Family, community, and other stakeholders are actively engaged and share responsibility for developing life-long learners.”
